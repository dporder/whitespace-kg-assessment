# Design. RM6116 into a knowledge graph

Dan Porder, 4 September 2026. This document is the reasoning behind the diagram in `diagram/`. The evaluation approach has its own document, `EVALUATION.md`. The material I handed to the coding agents is in `handover/`, and `CLAUDE.md` is the operating contract the agent fleet ran under.

## 1. What the graph is for

The shape of the questions decides what is worth extracting, so I started there. Every decision below is ultimately about queryability, meaning a human, or an agent acting for one, ends up holding the right information with proof of where it came from.

Five basic shapes cover most of what a system like this gets asked directly.

1. Point lookup with proof. "What does Clause 9.2 say", answered with the text, the page and the exact region on the page.
2. Obligation questions. "What must the Supplier do if a Default occurs", which needs the clause tree, the defined terms, and the references between them.
3. Impact questions. "What depends on Joint Schedule 11", which is the reverse closure over references, and is what an amendment assessment runs on.
4. Definition scoping. "What does Buyer System mean in this clause", which depends on where the definition lives and which schedule you are standing in.
5. Corpus questions, once there are thousands of documents. "Which of our frameworks cite the Bribery Act 2010", which only works if legislation references are normalised nodes rather than strings.

The sixth shape is the one real work arrives in, and it is messier than any of these. A buyer asks something like this. Our supplier on a networks call off has just filed accounts suggesting financial distress, part of the delivery runs through a subcontractor in another jurisdiction, and we are worried about continuity. What are our options, what notice do we owe, and what happens to exit if it comes to that. No single query answers it. An agent decomposes it into focused queries that hit different tiers of the graph, a concept filter to find the financial distress neighbourhood, term lookups for the defined machinery of default and termination, structural traversal down the relevant schedules, reference closure into exit management, and legislation nodes for the insolvency statutes behind it all. Each partial answer carries its citations, and the agent composes them without ever being the source of truth itself. The tiers below exist so that decomposition has somewhere sound to land, and I walk one of these scenarios end to end when I present this.

Anything that serves none of these shapes was cut.

## 2. Why legal documents need their own model

Four properties separate a contract from ordinary text, and each one shows up as a design decision below. Position changes meaning, the same words weigh differently depending on which schedule they sit in, so structure has to survive into the graph as structure. Capitalised terms override plain English, a Default is not a default, so the vocabulary is its own tier. Cross references are load bearing, a clause frequently cannot be understood without the clause it cites, so citations are first class objects rather than decoration. And nothing may be cleaned, a typo in a contract is part of the contract, so fidelity beats tidiness everywhere, and anything the pipeline infers is recorded alongside the untouched original rather than in place of it.

## 3. The data model, three tiers

The graph has three tiers with different trust levels and different costs. Keeping them separate is the core design decision, because it means a cheap deterministic layer is never contaminated by a probabilistic one, and every edge knows which kind it is. Other tiers could be added later, typed obligations is the obvious fourth, but each tier adds surface to evaluate and govern, so fewer tiers is more robust and three looks like the sweet spot for documents of this kind.

### Tier 1, structure. Deterministic.

The document tree. A document has parts, parts have sections and clauses, clauses have sub clauses and lettered sub paragraphs, and every one of those is its own node with its own page range and its own bounding boxes. Nothing is glued into a parent's text blob.

#### Where the node types come from

I did not want this ladder to be my invention, so it comes from two sources that agree with each other, the document's own stipulations and its observed behaviour.

The first is the document itself, which declares its own structural vocabulary in its interpretation clause. Joint Schedule 1, paragraph 1.3.8, says that references to Clauses and Schedules mean the clauses and schedules of the Core Terms, and that references in any Schedule to parts, paragraphs, annexes and tables mean those of the Schedule they appear in. Paragraph 1.3.9 says Paragraphs means the paragraph of the appropriate Schedule. So the document names its own units, Clause, Schedule, Part, Paragraph, Annex and Table, and it tells you which name applies where. Deriving the vocabulary from the interpretation clause rather than hardcoding it is the difference between a parser that works on this document and one that works on the family. It is worth being precise about why this is trusted while other supplied artifacts are not. The interpretation clause is operative contractual text, the parties agreed to it and a court would read the document through it, so it is ground truth about meaning. The bookmark tree and the page map are conveniences someone or something generated about the document, so they are cross checks to diff against, never inputs.

Vocabulary is inherited rather than global, which I checked rather than assumed. Joint Schedule 1 is the document level interpretation clause, and several schedules carry their own local ones on top of it. Call-Off Schedule 2 defines ten terms of its own, Call-Off Schedule 9 defines four, and Joint Schedule 11 and Call-Off Schedule 14 both open with "In this Schedule". Framework Schedule 1 has none at all and relies entirely on the document level. So resolution runs part local first and document level second, a locally defined term shadows the general one inside that part only, and the graph records which definition was used on each edge. A system that treated the definitions schedule as the single global dictionary would quietly return the wrong meaning inside those schedules.

That produces a fact worth building on. The same structural role carries a different label depending on which part you are standing in. Provision 9.2 of the Core Terms is a Clause. The provision with the same shape inside Call-Off Schedule 9 is a Paragraph. So structural nodes carry a `unit_label` property resolved per part, which means a citation renders as "Clause 9.2" in one place and "Paragraph 3.2" in another without a special case in the renderer. Getting that wrong is the kind of thing a lawyer notices immediately and an engineer never does.

The drafters do not follow their own rule perfectly, and the exceptions are where this stops being tidy. Across the pack there are 35 references of the form "Paragraph 1.x" and 27 of "paragraph N of this Schedule", against 3 that say "Clause 1.x" while sitting inside a Schedule. Those 3 are the whole problem in miniature. Read literally under paragraph 1.3.8, a reference to "Clause 1.2" points at Core Terms clause 1.2, but a drafter writing it inside a schedule may well have meant the local paragraph 1.2. There is no way to tell from the text alone. A resolver that applies the stipulated rule confidently gets them wrong and reports nothing, which is the exact failure the brief describes as worse than failing loudly. So they resolve to the stipulated target, carry status ambiguous with both candidates and the reason attached, and go to review. Three references out of roughly fifteen hundred is a rounding error until one of them is the clause someone is relying on.

The second is the observed numbering, which decides depth. Numbering runs to three dotted levels with lettered items below, and the depth is ragged. Clause 9.1 carries text and has lettered children, while 3.1 is a bare sub heading with no text of its own that exists only to group 3.1.1 and 3.1.2. That is why a node's kind is decided by what it does, holding text or holding children, rather than by counting dots in its number.

The floor of the ladder is the lettered or roman sub paragraph, because that is the deepest unit the document numbers and therefore the deepest unit anyone can cite. Across the pack there are 522 lettered and 82 roman items, and no numbering deeper than three dotted levels, so four addressable levels covers it.

Sentences are not a unit here, and I checked that rather than assuming it. Akoma Ntoso, the OASIS standard for legal documents, has no sentence element at all across its 315 element declarations. UK citation practice has no numbered form for one. The clearest evidence is a Court of Appeal judgment, Al Mana Lifestyle Trading v United Fidelity Insurance [2023] EWCA Civ 61, where the parties had to add their own bracketed numbers to a clause for argument, because the sentences inside it carried no identifier of their own. Splitting sentences would also be probabilistic on legal prose, which is dense with abbreviations and semicolon separated limbs, so it would put a guessed boundary inside the one tier that is meant to be deterministic. Where a UI wants to highlight a sentence, that is a character offset into a leaf, which is the mechanism references and term mentions already use.

Two things corroborate the branch or leaf rule from earlier. Akoma Ntoso models a container as an exclusive choice, either an optional intro, then children, then an optional wrap up, or a single content block, never both. The Office of the Parliamentary Counsel drafting guidance describes the same shape as a sandwich of opening words, paragraphs and closing words. Landing on that independently and then finding it twice attested is reassuring. RM6116 itself never uses closing words, so the profile supports them and the parser does not look for them.

One limit worth stating. The interpretation clause names Clause, Schedule, Part, Paragraph, Annex and Table, and says nothing at all about the lettered and roman items, which is most of the leaves. So vocabulary comes from the document for the units it names and from the profile for the ones it does not, and the graph records which source supplied each label. Deriving vocabulary from the document is the right default and it does not cover everything.

The kinds are not a closed list either. This family needs parts, headings, clauses, sub clauses, items, forms, tables and cells. Other families bring diagrams, figures with captions and legends, illustrations, or a collection level above the document when many instruments arrive bound as one file, which is in fact what RM6116 is, forty odd separately versioned templates in one binding. Kinds are declared per family in the hierarchy profile, so extending the set is configuration plus a fixture test, not a schema migration.

#### When a document does not fit the ladder

The ladder above is right for a UK public sector framework agreement and wrong for plenty of other things. A US style contract has Articles and Recitals. A technical manual has none of this. So the hierarchy is configuration rather than code. A `hierarchy_profile` is a rulebook describing how a family of documents is structured, the ordered unit names, the numbering grammar at each level, which units are citable, which kinds can occur, and the interpretation clause patterns to look for. The assembler stays a generic machine that reads a rulebook. Adding a document family is a config entry and a fixture test, not a parser rewrite.

The profiling stage then checks that the assigned rulebook actually fits, and refuses rather than guesses when it does not. Five signals, any of which quarantines the document with its evidence attached instead of ingesting it. No interpretation clause is found, or one is found naming units the rulebook does not have. The observed numbering does not match the rulebook's grammar. Too high a share of text blocks fail to attach to any node, which means the tree is wrong even where it parsed. Nesting depth falls outside the expected range. Or the indentation geometry disagrees with the numbering, since in a sane parse 3.1.2 sits further right on the page than 3.1, and when the numbers and the layout tell different stories the tree is probably mis built even though the numbers looked fine.

A quarantined document goes to a person with the specific mismatch shown, so the conversation is about which rulebook it needs rather than about why the graph looks strange. This is the one place I would rather fail loudly than proceed, since a confidently wrong hierarchy silently corrupts every citation built on top of it.

#### One node schema for everything that is ink on the page

There is a single `Node` schema, and a `kind` field does the differentiating, document, part, heading, preamble, clause, subclause, item, intro, form row, table, cell, and ref. All kinds share the same core fields, identity, path, labels, pages, boxes, ordering, anomalies, batch. A short table in the spec states which kinds fill which optional fields, enforced by validation, and the optional by kind fields are the accepted cost of one schema, one walker, one loader, one id scheme. Version metadata, author and dates where known, the source file name and hash, ingestion time and pipeline version, sits on the document root node, and `SUPERSEDES` edges run between roots when amendments arrive.

Cross references are the one kind that is a claim rather than anatomy. A ref is a place where the text of one provision cites another provision, schedule, definition or statute. It is a node whose parent is the citing provision, whose own text is the pointing words only, "Framework Schedule 4 (Framework Management)" as it appears in the citing sentence and never the target's content, whose location is character offsets into the parent's text, and which carries six extra fields, status, target, candidates, confidence, scope rule, resolver. It is marked not citable, since nobody cites a citation. A phrase that cites several targets, "a Default of Clauses 2.10, 9, 14, 15, 27", becomes one ref per cited target, each anchored to its own number's characters, sharing a group id. Ranges expand inclusively because the interpretation clause says series are inclusive. The children or text rule governs the anatomy partition, and a ref does not partition its parent, it annotates a span of it, so a leaf carries its text and its ref annotations at once without any duplication of content.

Why a node and not an edge. An edge needs both ends to exist, and the whole point here is that a citation can fail. Think of a payment rather than a wire, a link that can fail, hesitate, or need sign off graduates from edge to node, because it has a lifecycle of its own. An unresolved citation has no target to attach an edge to, and it must still exist, for the review queue, for the evaluation, and for the moment its target document finally arrives and it flips to resolved. The other rule that matters is that citations never mint nodes the document does not contain. A reference to a schedule that does not exist stays unresolved forever rather than conjuring a phantom target, because the graph asserts only what the ink asserts. And nearness for resolution is not character distance, the right metric is walking up the tree to the nearest enclosing part and applying the document's own stipulated scope rules, with raw proximity kept only as a scoring feature among candidates in the ambiguous residue. Akoma Ntoso models inline citations as elements of the document tree too, so there is one schema, and resolution state is just the mutable part of a ref node.

The document supplies its own scoping rules, as covered above, and the resolver implements paragraphs 1.3.8 and 1.3.9 directly. When a bare "Schedule 6" appears, the parenthetical title, as in "Schedule 6 (ICT Services)", disambiguates between the three schedule families, and when that fails the ref is marked ambiguous with its candidates attached rather than guessed.

Legislation references become `Legislation` nodes keyed on normalised title, year and, where the citation gives one, the provision inside the statute. Counting them across the document turns up 70 Act mentions, 18 Regulations mentions and 22 that point at a specific section, such as Sections 55 and 56 of the Patents Act 1977. So a legislation reference is not always a reference to a whole statute, and modelling it as one would lose the pointer that actually matters. Titles also carry parenthesised qualifiers that belong to the title, as in the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018, which is the sort of thing a normaliser truncates if nobody looked. Normalisation here never alters any stored text, it only mints the canonical key that lets fifty mentions of the same statute meet at one node. The raw pointing words stay on every ref untouched.

In this prototype that is where it stops. In production I would resolve these against the legislation.gov.uk identifier scheme, which addresses provisions directly, or a mirrored index of it inside an air gapped deployment, so that a claim an agent makes can be traced from answer to clause to the exact section of the exact statute. That chain is the thing a regulated client asks about first, and it only exists if legislation is a normalised node rather than a string sitting in some text.

Referent labels stay outside the tree, `Term`, `Legislation`, `Concept`. The full edge list is `CONTAINS`, `NEXT`, `RESOLVES_TO`, `CANDIDATE`, `USES_TERM`, `DEFINED_IN`, `ABOUT`, `SUPERSEDES`, plus `DEFINED_USING` between terms and `CONCEPT_REL` between concepts.

### Tier 2, vocabulary. Discovered by rule, matched deterministically.

Joint Schedule 1 defines roughly 300 capitalised terms, and the schedules define more inline. Each becomes a `Term` node with a `DEFINED_IN` edge to the provision that defines it, and every use in the text becomes a `USES_TERM` edge carrying the character span and a confident or ambiguous flag. Parties and roles, Supplier, Buyer, CCS, Relevant Authority, are terms, so this tier is also what gives the graph its actors. A term use is an edge rather than a node because its target always exists, which is exactly the property refs lack.

Declared and discovered vocabularies both run, and the graph records which a term came from. The declared list is the definitions schedule, which is document content and therefore must enter the graph regardless. The discovery rule runs independently over the whole document, legal drafting marks definitions by convention, a quoted capitalised phrase followed by "means" or "has the meaning", or the parenthetical form, so definition sites can be found without any list. Diffing the two produces three sets. Agreement, which needs no attention. Discovered but not declared, terms defined inline in schedules, which are wins a fixed list would have missed. And declared but not discovered, which are discovery misses to fix. Disagreements above a trickle are a conversation with a subject matter expert about which list is real, and for this document the declared schedule is treated as the source of truth while still being checked. The discovery rule is what scales to documents that ship no definitions schedule at all, and the honest caveat is that the convention is a drafting house style. I would test the rule on other CCS frameworks first, where it should transfer, then on a non CCS contract to find where it breaks.

Terms also carry abbreviations. The pack introduces them in the standard parenthetical way, the Crown Commercial Service (CCS), and after that point the abbreviation is the term. So the definition site parser captures parenthetical abbreviations at first use, stores them as aliases on the Term node, and the matcher matches aliases with the same rules as full forms. Missing this would silently drop most mentions of the most load bearing actor in the document.

The vocabulary also carries its own internal structure, deterministically. Definition texts are matched for term uses like any other text, so when the definition of Buyer Assets uses Buyer and Deliverables, the graph gets `DEFINED_USING` edges between the terms themselves. That is a dependency graph of the vocabulary, it costs nothing beyond the matching already being done, and its shape is diagnostic, a cycle or an unusually deep chain of definitions is exactly the kind of drafting artifact a reviewer should see.

Matching uses the vocabulary with three rules. Case sensitive exact match. Longest match wins, so Call-Off Contract beats Contract, and the reason is worth saying out loud, the longer string is always the more specific term, and a shorter term inside a longer match is not an independent use of the shorter term, it is a fragment of the longer one. And sentence initial or heading position marks the match ambiguous rather than confident, because that is where a capital letter stops being evidence.

Ambiguity is not one thing, and the router treats it as several. A sentence initial capital is one kind. A heading position is another. A third is text with high typo density, where capitalisation itself stops being trustworthy, because a stray capital can mint a false positive term use and a missing capital can hide a true one. Those are different error types with different costs, a false positive pollutes the graph and a false negative hides an obligation from a search, and the costs are not symmetric, which of the two is worse is a judgement a domain expert should weigh, not a default. So the pipeline computes a per section typo signal deterministically, spelling and obvious grammar checks, routes matches from high typo sections to their own narrow LLM check with a prompt specific to that failure mode, and the evaluation reports the confusion matrix with costs attached rather than bare accuracy. Different kinds of ambiguity go to different prompts, since a model asked "is this capital a sentence start" and a model asked "is this capital a typo" are answering different questions.

Routing only the self declared hard cases is not enough, because a systematic error in the easy cases would run silently. So a stratified random sample of confident matches is audited as well, stratified by term length, document part and match position so the sample matches the population rather than the convenient cases, in the same spirit as a golden set that mirrors the production distribution.

This matcher is the bootstrap, not the end state. The scale path is a small local NER model fine tuned on the matcher's confirmed output plus the corrections that come out of review, which removes the brittleness of casing conventions entirely and handles the typo cases natively. Which model family to start from, zero shot span extractors of the GLiNER kind against fine tuned token classifiers, is a research question I scoped into the agent workstream rather than guessed at, and the memo with that comparison sits in the repo.

### Tier 3, concepts. LLM derived, flagged as such.

An LLM reads each part and top level clause, with enough surrounding text to see how terms are actually used rather than isolated sentences, and returns the key concepts it is about and the relationships between those concepts. Every provision in that scope gets an `ABOUT` edge to its concepts, and concept to concept edges form a small side ontology. Concepts are resolved with embedding similarity and clustering so that near duplicates collapse, the same move as entity resolution in my previous graph work. Every concept node carries an `llm_derived` flag and a confidence. Concepts never duplicate the vocabulary, a proposed concept whose label collides with a declared term, exactly, by alias, or by embedding near duplicate, is not minted, the collision is logged and the term keeps the job, because a deterministic tier 2 object always outranks a generated tier 3 one.

This tier exists for queryability. An agent answering the scenario in section 1 starts by filtering the graph to the provisions tagged with a concept like termination triggers or supplier insolvency risk, neighbourhoods no defined term names, then runs its precise queries inside that neighbourhood. That first narrowing step is what makes agentic retrieval over a large graph tractable, and it is the part a pure structure graph cannot offer. At corpus scale the concept scan moves from a frontier model to a small language model fine tuned for this narrow tagging task on this domain, which is the same bootstrap then specialise path as the term matcher.

The rule that keeps this tier honest is that concepts are navigation, never citation. An answer cites provisions, pages and boxes from tier 1. Concepts only help an agent or a human find the right neighbourhood. This is also why the tier can afford to be probabilistic while the tiers below it are not.

### Salience, an attribute rather than a tier

Some provisions matter more than others, and retrieval should know it. Every structural node and every term carries a salience score computed deterministically at load time, and the shape of the function matters more than its constants, breadth linear, frequency log damped. For a provision, salience is the number of distinct parts whose refs resolve to it, multiplied by the log of one plus the resolved citation count. For a term, the same with parts that use it and use count. I have shipped this exact asymmetric shape in a production graph, and the reason it works is that raw frequency is a trap, the most repeated string in a corpus is usually boilerplate, while being needed from many distinct places is real evidence of load bearing. Repeated furniture, headers, footers, form placeholders, is excluded from the counts entirely, and anything whose frequency is wildly out of distribution for its kind gets flagged for eyes rather than boosted. At query time salience enters as a small additive log boost on top of relevance scores, sized so that relevance always dominates and salience only breaks ties. It is a ranking aid for retrieval and review prioritisation, never a statement of legal weight, and because it is a pure function of the graph it recomputes for free on every load.

### What is deliberately not modelled yet

- Typed obligations. Legal informatics calls these deontic categories, who must do what, who may, who must not, under what condition and by when. Question shape 2 ultimately wants them as nodes, an obligation with an obligor, an action, a condition and a deadline, extracted per provision. It is the obvious fourth tier and the first thing I would add, and it is deferred because it deserves its own evaluation set rather than a rushed one.
- Temporal validity and point in time queries. The versioning design below makes them possible later. Modelling them now would double the schema for no answerable question tonight.
- Automated precedence between conflicting clauses. The honest position is that a heuristic guessing which clause wins would be wrong often enough to be dangerous. What the graph does instead is make conflict visible and decidable by a person, both answers surface with their provenance, which version, which author where known, how recent, what cites them, and when the set of conflicting answers is large, ranking and an agent compress it, with the full set always one click deeper. Authority to resolve a conflict stays with the human, the graph's job is to make sure nothing relevant is hidden.
- Executed call offs. RM6116 is a template, the framework everyone signs up to. When a buyer actually procures under it, a filled in contract is created from these templates, with real parties, dates and prices in the cells that say "[Insert name]" here. Those executed documents are future siblings in the graph, each referencing the framework version it was formed under. The model leaves them room and the prototype does not pretend to have them.

## 4. Identity, provenance and idempotency

A node's identity is document, version, part, then path, for example `rm6116/v3.0.11/core-terms/3/3.1/3.1.2/a`. Never the bare clause number, because numbering restarts in every part, and the same string "paragraph 2.2" names different things in different schedules. The clause number is still stored and indexed as a property, so a person can search by "9.2" and filter from there, identity and lookup are different jobs.

Two keys come off that path. The `lineage_key` is a hash of the document and path with the version left out, so it names clause 3.1.2(a) of RM6116 as a thing that persists across amendments. The node `id` is a hash of the full path including the version, so it names this version's instance of that clause, holding that version's text, pages and boxes. Instances sharing a lineage key are that clause's version history, ask for a lineage key and you get every version of that one clause in order, which is exactly the clause level version chain you want without giving up whole document versioning. Each instance stores a hash of its own normalised text, so a version diff reports changed, unchanged, added and removed without re reading the PDF. Hashing rather than using the path string directly is convenience rather than correctness, paths carry slashes and spaces that need escaping everywhere they travel, and a fixed width key indexes better. The readable path stays on the node as a property, because that is what a person wants to see in a query result.

Provenance is layered, and the document root carries the file layer. Source file name and content hash, file system and PDF metadata dates and author where present, ingestion timestamp, pipeline version, and which extraction route each page took. Each version instance records who made it and when, where that is knowable, for this specimen the authorship is the template version in each part's footer, for a client corpus it is the amendment's author, and the first version of every provision inherits the document's original authorship. Beyond the file there is context provenance that matters in regulated settings and cannot be derived from the bytes, owning department, custodian, where the document is distributed, and its access classification. Those are ingestion time metadata fields on the document root, supplied by the source system or the person loading it, and the access classification is what row level security keys off later.

Deterministic ids are what make re ingestion safe. The loader uses MERGE against a uniqueness constraint on the id, so a second run over the same input finds the existing node and updates its properties instead of creating a twin. That is what upsert means here, update if present and insert if absent, and it is the thing that prevents duplication rather than causing it. Ids minted at ingestion time, a UUID or a counter, would create a second node for the same clause on every run and silently double the graph.

Upsert on its own is not the whole story, and three gaps are worth naming. A node the first run created and the second run no longer asserts would otherwise sit there orphaned, so each load is scoped and swept, deleting anything under that scope carrying the previous batch tag that this batch did not re assert. Relationship merges need their own keys or a rerun grows parallel edges between the same pair. And the generative stages are not deterministic, so their ids stay stable while their confidences and candidate lists drift between runs, which is why every model call is logged and replayable from a cache keyed on its input. The deterministic stages are idempotent by construction. The LLM stages are idempotent only if you pin them. Merges, deduplications and deletions are all events worth an audit trail, every one lands in a log with the batch that caused it, what it affected and why, so the graph's history is reconstructable and a bad decision is findable and reversible rather than silent.

Every provision carries the absolute PDF page range, the printed page number from the part's own footer, which restarts per part and is what a lawyer would quote, and two sets of bounding boxes. One covers the provision's own text, the other covers the provision and everything nested under it, each as one box per page touched. Overlap between a parent's extent and its children is expected, and it is useful, because it lets a viewer highlight either a whole clause or one lettered sub paragraph without walking the tree first. Every claim downstream can therefore be proven by rendering the page and drawing the box.

Boxes are cheap here because this PDF is a native text layer, every glyph already carries its position, so extracting a box is reading numbers the file already holds. Other inputs are not so kind, and the design covers them with a triage ladder rather than one path. A page with a text layer takes the deterministic route. A page without one, a scan or a photograph, is rendered and goes to a layout OCR model that returns text with word level boxes. Pages where those two disagree, or where tables and figures defeat the layout model, escalate to a vision model as the expensive last resort. Whatever the route, the page image itself is stored as an artifact and linked from the page, so a consumer can always look at the actual scan beside the extracted text, and every node records which route produced it so confidence can be discounted downstream where the extraction stood on weaker ground.

Text does not work the same way. Raw text lives only on leaf nodes, and the full text of a clause plus its sub paragraphs is a derived view rather than a stored field. Three things break if text is copied up the tree. The same sentence lands in the graph at three levels, so a vector search returns the same passage as three different hits and a citation cannot say which level actually matched. A change to one lettered paragraph dirties the content hash of every ancestor, so the version diff reports a whole clause as amended when one line moved. And references and term uses are stored as character offsets into a node's text, which stop being unambiguous once the same sentence exists in several nodes. The search index is free to hold denormalised text at whatever granularity retrieval wants, because the index is not the graph.

So the rule is that a node has anatomy children or it has text, and never both. Where the source gives a clause a lead in sentence and then sub paragraphs, as clause 9.1 does with its "to enable it to both" followed by (a) and (b), the lead in becomes its own child of kind `intro`, marked not independently citable. That marking mirrors legal reality rather than fighting it, nobody cites opening words on their own, a lawyer says clause 9.1 and means the lead in together with its paragraphs, which is exactly what rendering the parent produces, the intro read in order with its siblings. The intro node is how the lead in gets its own offsets, boxes and hash without breaking the one rule that keeps text unambiguous, and citations to 9.1 resolve to the parent, whose derived view includes it. The legislative XML standards landed on the same shape for the same reason. The cost is one extra node per container that has a lead in, and it buys back a clean partition of every character on the page.

The bottom of that hierarchy is the deepest unit the document itself numbers, which here is the lettered sub paragraph. The footers also carry per part template versions, Core Terms at v3.0.11 and each schedule with its own Model Version, which is evidence that the pack is an assembly of separately versioned templates. The graph stores that per part version, because it is what an amendment will arrive against.

Every node and edge carries the `batch_id` of the ingestion run that created it. A batch can be rolled back in one operation, which is the cheap insurance that makes continuous ingestion survivable. This pattern, and the composite uniqueness constraints behind it, I carried over from the loader of a graph product I built end to end at Valae, my previous agency, where the same mechanism scopes tenants rather than versions.

## 5. The pipeline

Nine stages. Each is a separate CLI with a JSON contract on both sides, so stages can be rerun, tested and swapped independently. The expensive and non deterministic stages are pushed as late as possible, and the stage graph is wider than it is long, after assembly the reference, vocabulary, concept and embedding stages all run in parallel per part, load is the join, and parts themselves fan out from the start, so wall clock is set by the slowest part rather than the sum.

**Stage 0, profile.** Free, instant. For each document and page, does a text layer exist, is the PDF tagged, does an outline exist, what does the font size distribution look like, which rulebook fits, and do the five fit checks pass or quarantine. Font size earns its place twice. Where explicit numbering exists it is a cross check, a heading rendered smaller than the body below it is an anomaly worth flagging even when the numbers parse. Where the text layer is poor or absent, size, weight and colour become the primary structure signal, which is exactly how documents without a numbering grammar get a usable hierarchy. The profile also routes each page down the extraction ladder, deterministic text layer first, layout OCR where there is none, vision models only where cheaper rungs disagree or fail, cost and latency rising with each rung. The route taken is stored per page on the document root so downstream consumers know what kind of extraction their answers stand on.

**Stage 1, parse.** Deterministic, PyMuPDF. Words with boxes per page, headers and footers stripped by position and repetition, part boundaries detected from the per part title and footer signature changes, numbering captured with its box. Tables and forms are detected and kept as tables with cell boxes rather than flattened to prose, and the kinds registry leaves room for figures, captions and illustrations in families that have them. Output is one layout file per part, every block carrying text, page and boxes.

**Stage 2, assemble.** Deterministic. The flat numbered lines become the tree, relationships made explicit, this item belongs to that clause, that clause to its heading, siblings in reading order. The assembler enforces the invariants, children nest under their numeric parents, siblings ascend, no gaps without a recorded exception, indentation agrees with numbering, and every violation is logged rather than repaired silently, because the violations are themselves a quality signal. Where a violation looks like a typo, a number missing its period, the anomaly can carry an LLM proposed reading with a confidence, recorded beside the raw text and never in place of it, for a human to confirm. The Award Form and parts like it assemble as forms, numbered rows of label and value cells, rather than clause trees.

**Stage 3, references.** Two separately specified and separately evaluated steps, detection then resolution. Detection is a citation grammar, an anchor unit word, Clause, Paragraph, Schedule, Annex, Part, Section, Act, Regulations, followed by a number and list grammar, each number in a list phrase becoming its own ref anchored to its own characters with a shared group id. Behind the grammar runs an orphan keyword detector, any unit word not covered by a detected span is surfaced for triage as either generic prose use or a missed citation, so the grammar's recall is measured rather than assumed. The fallback ladder is grammar, then orphan scan, then LLM span extraction over orphan sentences only, then the review queue. Anaphoric forms, this Schedule, that Clause, are detected by pattern and resolved only by LLM or human, never by the grammar. Resolution then applies the document's stipulated scope rules to each detected ref, and the residue the rules cannot settle, bare schedule numbers without titles, the mislabelled Clause cases, anaphora, goes to an LLM shown the top candidates together with explicit permission to answer none, and none keeps the ref unresolved with its candidates attached. The two layer contract, deterministic pass stamping its method, model only on survivors, candidates presented together, none as a valid answer, confidence recorded, is lifted directly from an incremental entity matcher I built into Valae's knowledge pipelines, where the deterministic layer covered about 60 percent of cases at effectively perfect precision.

**Stage 4, vocabulary.** As described in tier 2. Declared list ingestion, discovery by rule, the diff between them, alias capture, matching with the three rules, typed ambiguity routing, typo density signal, stratified audit sampling.

**Stage 5, concepts.** As described in tier 3. Per section LLM scan over long context windows, embedding resolution of near duplicates, ABOUT edges, concept relations. Runs in parallel with stages 3, 4 and 6, it shares inputs with them and none of them reads its output.

**Stage 6, embeddings and summaries.** Retrieval needs vectors at more than one altitude, because a query like "who owns the intellectual property" should land on clause 9 while a query quoting a phrase should land on the leaf that contains it. Leaves get their raw text embedded, which is precise and deterministic. Containers get two vectors, one over the concatenated text of their subtree where that fits a sensible token budget, and one over a short generated summary. The concatenation keeps a container findable by its own words, and the summary is what gives altitude, since an embedding averaged over a very long clause drifts toward nothing in particular. Summaries are only generated where they earn their cost, whole documents, parts, and containers too long to embed directly, a leaf's raw text is already the best embedding input it has. One variant worth testing rather than assuming, embedding each leaf with a window of its neighbours, the clause before and after, sometimes buys semantically richer retrieval in my experience, it would replace the plain leaf embedding rather than double storage, and the evaluation section says how the comparison would be scored.

Two rules keep this from leaking into the trust gradient. A summary is generated text, so it sits in the same trust class as a concept, and a hit on a summary vector has to resolve down to leaf text before anything is cited from it. And the vectors live in a search index keyed by node id rather than on the graph nodes themselves, which lets the corpus be re embedded on a new model without touching the graph, and lets retrieval scale separately from storage. That is the same split I moved to in my own product, for the same reasons.

**Stage 7, load.** Neo4j through MERGE only, constraints first, batch tagged, rollback able, the sweep making reruns converge. A JSON export of the same graph ships alongside, so nothing about the design depends on the engine, and the same loader contract would target ArangoDB or Neptune. Access control is designed in rather than bolted on, every node carries the access classification inherited from its document, so role based access becomes a filter the query layer enforces, different roles see different subgraphs of one graph, which is both the sovereign requirement and the economical way to run mixed sensitivity corpora on shared infrastructure. Operationally the graph is never a single copy, identical replicas serve staging and testing, snapshots are taken on every batch and kept on separate infrastructure, a different region or different on premise hardware, because in the environments this is for, durability against failure and against attack is part of the design and not an afterthought. In a sovereign deployment the whole pipeline runs inside the boundary, the only outbound dependency being the model endpoint, which is exactly the component Whitespace's platform exists to bring inside.

**Stage 8, evaluate.** Runs after every load, detailed in `EVALUATION.md`. Structural invariants, the cross checks against the provided artifacts, the page map, the outline, the definitions list, each derived independently and diffed, the golden sets for references and terms with detection and resolution scored separately, the stratified audits, and the batch transition counts. Evaluation cost scales with scope, so a routine load evaluates the parts it touched, and scheduled sweeps run the full battery over the whole graph on a cadence rather than on every load.

Cost and failure profile in one line each. Stages 0 to 2 are free, deterministic, and fail loudly on malformed layout. Stage 3 is deterministic for most references, pennies of LLM for the residue, and its failure mode is a wrong resolution, which is why confidence and candidates are stored. Stage 4 is free apart from the routed ambiguity checks. Stages 5 and 6 are the LLM spend, throttleable, skippable, per part parallel, and they are investments in queryability rather than in fidelity, the graph is already correct without them, they make it findable. Stage 7 is I O bound. Nothing in the hot path is a fine tuned model, so there is nothing to retrain when the corpus changes, and the fine tuned specialists that arrive later, the term matcher, the concept tagger, slot into stages 4 and 5 behind the same contracts.

## 6. When the document fights back

- No table of contents, no trustworthy outline. Nothing depends on either. This document ships a 498 entry outline, and the pipeline treats it purely as a cross check to diff against, because sampling showed it degrades below the top level in exactly the schedules whose source styling was sloppiest. The diff triages disagreements into parser wrong, outline wrong, or both, per part.
- Scanned or degraded pages. The extraction ladder from section 4 takes over, and its rungs come from a ladder I have run in production on far messier corpora than this. Cheap per page statistics, character count, image area fraction, table count, font histogram, classify each page and route it. A table safety net cross checks the model's output against a deterministic table detector and re extracts any table the fast path missed, from a high resolution crop of just that region. Tables that continue across a page break are detected by their positions against the page edges and re extracted as one table rather than two fragments. Thin results from scan like pages trigger tiled re extraction at higher zoom with overlap stitching. Every model call sits inside a fallback chain that ends at the deterministic parser rather than at an exception, including the content filter false positives that sensitive corpora trip constantly. And the page image itself is stored and linked from the page node through a per document manifest, so a consumer can always see the scan beside the extracted text, and provenance records the route so confidence can be discounted where the ground was weaker.
- Inconsistent numbering. Assembly invariants log every violation. The pack contains real ones, a stray character in a form label, "rFramework", and a heading whose number detached from its period, and the right behaviour is recording the exception with an optional model proposed reading beside the raw text, never a silent repair. Absolutely nothing is cleaned in a legal corpus, the document is represented at maximum fidelity including its errors, and any interpretive layer sits beside the original, flagged as interpretation. In non legal corpora a cleaning pass for queryability can be defensible, and even there the untouched ground truth stays retrievable underneath.
- Forms. Some parts are not clauses at all but fill in forms, numbered rows pairing a label with a value, here the Award Form with its "[Insert name]" placeholders, which are preserved exactly because in an executed contract those cells hold the parties and the money. The same principle covers the pathological cases, a column header reading "Note to Jon, fill this in" is recorded as the column header it physically is, and flagged as unlikely to be a real header, both facts in the graph, fidelity and usability carried separately rather than one overwriting the other.
- Tables and multi column layouts. Cells are nodes with their own boxes, column detection runs on box geometry rather than line order, which is what makes the two column definitions schedule parse even though its terms wrap mid cell across lines. None of this is specific to how this document happens to draw its tables, the shapes are declared in the family rulebook, and a new family with stranger furniture extends the rulebook rather than the parser.

## 7. Scale. The second, hundredth and ten thousandth document

**The second document** is the interesting one, and the build demonstrates it live. I ingest the slice as four successive batches, Core Terms first, then the definitions schedule, then the Award Form, then a call off schedule. After batch one, references out to schedules that have not arrived yet sit unresolved. When their targets land in later batches, the resolver re runs over dangling refs and they flip to resolved, with the transition counted in the eval report. That is the amendment and arrival story in miniature, and it falls out of refs being nodes with status rather than edges that either exist or do not.

**The hundredth** is about idempotency and money. Deterministic ids make re ingestion an upsert. Batch tags make any load reversible. The LLM spend is confined to the reference residue and the concept and summary tiers, all throttleable, and everything else is CPU. Per part parallelism is trivial because parts are independent until reference resolution, which is a join at the end.

**The ten thousandth** is about variety and drift. New drafting houses break the definition convention and the numbering grammar, which is why both are versioned rulebooks evaluated per document, and why the profile stage exists to route or quarantine rather than assume. A corpus also stops being a pile of independent files at this scale, documents arrive in collections, a framework and its call offs, a case and its bundle, and the model's collection level exists for exactly that grouping. Amendments arrive as new versions of a document root with a `SUPERSEDES` edge. The tree diffs against the previous version by lineage key and content hash, so an untouched provision is recognised as the same provision across versions even though each version holds its own instance of it, and a point in time query is a filter on version rather than a separate store. The cost is real, storage grows with versions times provisions, and the intended optimisation at scale is copy on write, mint a new instance only where the content hash changed and let unchanged provisions span versions on a validity range. I would measure before switching, because the simple model is far easier to defend in an audit. What breaks first, honestly, is the reference resolver's precision on cross document references, when "Schedule 2" could belong to any of ten thousand frameworks, and the answer is that resolution becomes scoped search within the citing document's collection plus a review queue, not a bigger model.

## 8. The human in the loop

Two places, chosen because they compound. First, the review queue. Every ambiguous or unresolved ref, every ambiguous term use, every anomaly with a proposed reading lands in a small UI showing the source sentence, the page image with the box drawn, and the candidates. The reviewer picks or rejects. Second, the same decisions become the golden set. Every human decision is a labelled example, so the eval set grows as a by product of operating the system rather than as a separate annotation project, and the fine tuned matchers train on exactly those labels later. A reviewer's hour spent unblocking today is also an hour of ground truth for regression testing forever. The derived versus provided diffs feed the same loop, where the pipeline's tree disagrees with a supplied artifact, a sampled subset goes to a person who says which side is right, or neither, and those verdicts tune the derivers so the disagreement rate falls with use, measured on documents the tuning never saw.

## 9. The build slice, and why

Core Terms, pages 1 to 22, the floor the brief names, plus the definitions schedule, the Award Form and Call-Off Schedule 9, about 80 pages, run as the four incremental batches described above. The definitions schedule is what makes the vocabulary tier real, the Award Form proves the structural model on a part that is a form with tables rather than clauses, and Call-Off Schedule 9 brings references that cross a document boundary, which is where the notes say resolution stops being trivial, and I agree. If time compresses the build, Core Terms and the Award Form are the two that must run, structure proven on both shapes of content, and the other two follow in order. The deterministic stages alone also run across all 475 pages at no LLM cost, producing the derived page map, the outline diff and per part invariant violations, which is the concrete evidence for what would and would not survive the rest of the document.

One check that run settles. The notes say 46 constituent parts, the provided page map lists 48 rows, and my derived map reports what the document itself supports.

## 10. Models per stage

My default is the smallest model that survives the eval, chosen per stage, with the judge a different model from the extractor.

| Stage | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2, 4 profile, parse, assemble, vocabulary | none | Deterministic. |
| 3 reference residue | Claude Haiku 4.5 | Bounded choice among presented candidates. Escalate single hard cases to Sonnet 5. |
| 5 concepts | Claude Sonnet 5 | Open generation, needs judgement, not frontier cost. At corpus scale, a fine tuned small model. |
| 6 summaries | Claude Haiku 4.5 | Compression, not reasoning. |
| Embeddings | OpenAI text-embedding-3-large | Retrieval quality is worth a strong embedding model. In a sovereign deployment an in boundary embedding model replaces it behind the same interface, vectors live outside the graph precisely so that swap is a re embed, not a migration. |
| Eval judge | Claude Haiku 4.5, separate prompt | Independence from the extractor matters more than judge size, the human golden set anchors it. |
| Chat agent | Claude Opus 5 | The demo surface, tool use quality dominates. |

## 11. Thought through, guessed, would prototype first

**Thought through.** The three tier model and its trust gradient. Refs as nodes with status and a lifecycle. Identity, lineage and idempotency keys. The scope rules, which come from the document itself. Batch tagging, rollback and the sweep. The cross checks against the provided artifacts. The staged ingestion demonstration.

**Guessed.** That the definition discovery convention transfers across CCS frameworks. That term matching ambiguity concentrates in single word terms in heading position. That concept resolution needs only embedding clustering at this corpus size. Each guess is written next to the evaluation that would confirm or kill it.

**Would prototype before committing.** The structural diff between document versions, which looks clean on paper and is notoriously fiddly on real amendments. Cross document reference resolution at corpus scale. The neighbour window embedding variant, which has helped me before but needs an A B retrieval eval here. The fine tuned term matcher and concept tagger, where the open questions are training data volume and starting model family, scoped as a research task in the agent workstream with the memo in the repo.
